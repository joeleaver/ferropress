A Squall Off Cape Horn

The barometer had been falling since noon, and by four bells the mate had already doubled the watch. Grey water climbed the rail in long, unhurried swells, the kind that promise more than they show.

We took in the topgallants before the light went, and it was well we did — the wind came round out of the southwest inside the hour, and with it a sea that turned the whole ship into a single working animal, every line and timber straining together.

There is a particular quiet that settles over a crew in weather like that. No one speaks beyond what the work requires. The bosun's calls carry further than they should, flattened by the wind into something almost conversational.

By the middle watch the squall had blown itself out as quickly as it came, leaving the sea confused and the sky scrubbed clean. Cape Horn does that — gives you an hour to remember it by and calls it even.

Recommendation Ship the responsive split as ruled: below-sheet at normal widths, promoted to a 19rem .editor__aside rail at ≥1280px. That threshold (not the .wrap's own 68rem/1088px breakpoint) leaves headroom for the sheet's 34rem measure plus its 3rem padding to stay centered-ish rather than getting shoved hard left the moment the rail appears. Below 1280px the stacked layout reads better than a cramped rail would. The panel content and every class inside it is identical in both slots — only the grid wrapper differs — so the rinch port is one if viewport_is_wide around which <div>/<aside> wraps the same panel markup, not two implementations to keep in sync.

Found by testing this frame with real content: a real post body is usually much taller than two taxonomy panels, so a static aside would end its short column and leave a tall dead zone of paper beside the rest of the sheet. .editor__aside is therefore position: sticky; top: 1rem (toggle to wide and scroll the sheet to see it hold) — the panels stay in view the whole time you're writing, instead of scrolling out of reach after the first screenful.

Implementation notes

  • Rows in .termcheck__row are label > input[type=checkbox] + span per SF6 — never a <button> with a glyph checkbox (rinch dispatches onclick from pointerdown only, which is mouse-only). Each panel is a real fieldset/legend.
  • .chip is a deliberately new family, not a reuse of .tag (SF7): .tag is an uppercase mono kind-stamp where the dashed border already means "targets a term" (menu rows); reusing it for a category/tag name would both shout the name in caps and overload the dashed language with a second, conflicting meaning ("pending create" here vs "is a term" there).
  • .trow (§C) is visually identical to the existing .mrow (menu list rows) — same grid shape, same hover treatment. Worth just reusing .mrow directly rather than shipping a near-duplicate class; the only difference is the count column, which can be a slotted child either way.
  • The combobox list only appears when there's a matching real vocab entry to suggest — a buffer that matches nothing renders no list at all (not an empty listbox), so Enter unambiguously commits the typed text as a staged chip.
  • role="status" live region (see the hidden node under §A's normal-width frame) is where a save/delete outcome gets announced per SF16 — today's toast is visual-only and announces nothing.
  • The breakpoint toggle above is the only interactive JS in this file, on purpose (see the file's header comment) — every other "state" is a separate static section, not a JS-driven variant, so a reviewer can see all of them at once without clicking through.

Setting the Register: Static-First Delivery in Practice

JC By Jane Compositor

Most content systems treat rendering as a runtime tax: every reader pays for the page to be assembled again. Ferropress takes the opposite position. A page is set once, proved, and served as static bytes — and it is composed again only when a change actually touches it.

That single decision is the difference between a press and a photocopier. The photocopier reproduces on demand; the press sets the forme, and every impression after is nearly free.

The register subscription

The regeneration loop rides rhypedb's change stream. When an author publishes, edits, or trashes a post, exactly the affected pages are re-proved — never the whole publication. The same stream doubles as the cache-invalidation broadcast across instances.

Keep WordPress's authoring soul; modernize the delivery. The reader gets a static page; the author keeps the sheet.

What stays live

Not everything is frozen. Three things are composed at the moment of the request, so a global change never forces a rebuild of every page:

  • the masthead — your site title and tagline, read from settings;
  • the dateline — each date formatted to your chosen pattern and timezone;
  • the islands — search and live comments, hydrated client-side.

Everything else — the words you are reading — was set at publish time and pulled straight from the cache. The body of this article is styled by the very same rules that draw it in the editor, so the proof on the sheet is the page on the press.

One shared renderer

There is exactly one place a block tree becomes HTML. The editor preview and this public page both call it, which is the whole reason the two can never drift:

let body = render_with(&tree, RenderMode::Publish, plugins);
// same crate the editor calls — WYSIWYG by construction

The result is a publication that reads fast, holds its register, and never surprises the person who set it. That is the entire promise: modern delivery, WordPress's soul.
